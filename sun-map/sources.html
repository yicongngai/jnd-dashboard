Sources and method

Version 24 · 21 September 2026. This tool supports explanations of sun direction and approximate ground shading. It does not certify sunlight at a particular flat.

Sun position

Astronomy Engine 2.1.19 computes a topocentric geometric solar position at the study centre, with elevation set to zero. The date is interpreted in Singapore time, UTC+8, regardless of the device’s timezone. Compass bearings start at true north and increase clockwise.

Validation against the independent pvlib implementation of NREL SPA: 495 cases at three Singapore locations, in five dates spanning the year across 2024, 2026 and 2030. Maximum geometric altitude difference: 0.000446°. Maximum angular separation of solar directions: 0.000447°. This validates the calculation in these cases, not the completeness or accuracy of building data. Fifteen sunrise/set pairs differ by at most 31 seconds; the libraries use different solar-disc and refraction approximations.

Block addresses and sun-path guide

Version 24 retains 71,376 full address records from the public OpenStreetMap extract and established HDB crosswalks. Addresses are matched by a unique outline overlap of at least 80%, a reviewed replacement identity, or an established official HDB address match. Addresses are loaded regardless of whether a building already has a height. Parts may inherit an address from their explicit parent; no nearest-neighbour address guesses are made. Missing addresses are labelled as unavailable. These are source records, not independently verified postal addresses.

The gold sun-path arc shows compass directions from sunrise to sunset for the study centre and selected day. Its radius is a display scale, not solar distance or altitude. Click sunrise, sunset or an hour label to set the study time. The moving sun marker follows the time slider; the altitude chart shows the sun’s height above the horizon. The path can be hidden using its map control.

Building geometry and heights

  • Building and building-part footprints: Overture’s public building tiles, release 2026-08-19.0, downloaded 16 September 2026 Singapore time. The 860-tile regional envelope includes offshore islands and neighbouring territory for shadow buffers; it is not a national administrative boundary. Expanded coverage includes Pulau Satumu, eastern Pulau Tekong and Pedra Branca. Full geographic coverage does not establish that every footprint or height is present. The source tiles simplify and clip geometry; clipped fragments sharing a stable ID are merged before modelling.
  • Sources include OpenStreetMap, Microsoft ML Buildings and Google Open Buildings. Satellite-derived roofprints may differ from the ground footprint. Source dates vary and appear in building details. Buildings erected or removed since those dates may be incorrect.
  • A recorded source height is labelled as recorded, not surveyed or independently verified. Storey counts are multiplied by 3 m only when the estimate option is enabled. Real floor heights, podiums and roof structures vary.
  • CDL confirms Amber Park’s three 21-storey towers. This corrects their missing storey data. It does not establish an exact 63 m height. CDL’s elevation above sea level is not used as height above ground.
  • Unclassified footprints with missing heights remain flat. Source-classified landed homes and their linked parts are flat and cast no shadow. Elevated parts with ambiguous roof elevations are excluded until a roof height above ground is supplied. Overall outlines with separate parts do not become uniform towers.
  • A manual edit means the user’s roof height above local ground. It is retained in this page session, including when revisiting the location, and is lost when the page reloads.

Shadow model and limits

The model treats each footprint as an opaque, flat-roof prism on a horizontal plane. Shadow length is height ÷ tan(solar altitude), directed away from the sun. Coordinate offsets use WGS84 local metres per degree. Boolean operations use a local integer millimetre grid to avoid numerical failures at coincident edges. Polygon unions handle overlapping shadows, concave footprints and courtyards.

The study follows the visible neighbourhood, with a 120 m overlap margin and a 2.25 km surrounding caster buffer. Shadows are clipped to the study boundary. Calculations are suppressed below 5° solar altitude, or a higher threshold when the tallest loaded building needs it. A missing required tile prevents a partial study from being shown.

The model excludes terrain and differences in ground level, roof slopes, overhangs, balconies, vegetation, weather, indoor spaces, glazing and shadows on façades. Geometric solar altitude excludes atmospheric refraction for the shadow projection. Unknown heights may conceal substantial shading. Unshaded ground does not prove direct sunlight.

Automatic coverage and future projects

The visible map loads automatically at neighbourhood scale (zoom 15 and closer). A 2.25 km surrounding buffer supplies shadow casters. Island overview shows project markers; zoom in for shadows. Near the dataset edge, shadows stop at the explicitly clipped coverage boundary.

The completed-development scenario contains 97 building sections across 20 projects, reviewed against vault site plans, elevation charts and official HDB brochures. Forty-five other catalogue entries have no modelled future shadows; nine also lack usable coordinates. Reviewed geographic source outlines retain their source positions; other models use approximate project-centred alignment. Heights use 3 m per storey. Missing models are not evidence of unobstructed sunlight. See project coverage and source plans.

The future toggle changes the building scenario independently of the selected solar date. Existing snapshot footprints within a modelled project site are replaced when future mode is on. Podiums and ancillary structures are generally omitted.

Source coverage audit

A complete OpenStreetMap extract via Geofabrik, through 15 September 2026 20:20 UTC, supplies 3,255 matched records: 25 source-recorded heights and 3,229 storey counts. Current outline overlap must pass an 80% intersection-over-union check, and ambiguous matches, elevated structures and parents with separate parts are excluded. Source-recorded heights take priority over supplementary floor estimates; official HDB counts take priority over OSM floor counts. The two supplements cover 4,186 objects. The latest outline corrections allow source storeys to be applied to 179 previously missing-height objects; these use the same 3 m per storey estimate. Existing tile heights and storeys remain unchanged.

All 123,029 footprints in Microsoft’s Singapore release of 13 August 2026 were geometrically compared with the Overture snapshot. 12,731 outlines of at least 12 m² had less than 20% overlap. These are unverified differences, not a confirmed count of missing buildings: roof displacement, source age and detection errors require review. Microsoft supplies no positive Singapore heights in this release. These candidate outlines have not been silently inserted into the map.

Current OpenStreetMap outlines now correct 807 existing footprints and add 105, including 18 explicitly tagged landed homes. Native source metadata is preserved on corrected footprints; three individually verified HDB floor-count corrections are applied separately at display time; new footprints use source heights or storeys where available, with landed homes kept flat. Underground objects, possible future sites, ambiguous building parts, conflicting storeys, large changes and overlapping candidates are held for review. These source outlines are not surveyed positions.

These are HDB maximum floor levels, converted to approximate heights using 3 m per storey. Lower wings and roof structures remain unresolved. Another 343 have partial overlap and 95 have less than 20%. Some source outlines describe unbuilt projects without a construction tag. Of the 438 differences, 41 have at least 80% coverage from reviewed future models, leaving 397 without such coverage. These are source differences, not a count of missing physical buildings. Area coverage can hide a misplaced neighbouring footprint and does not establish correct individual alignment.

None of these comparisons proves complete real-world coverage.

Original-resolution source audit

The original Overture GeoParquet for the same 2026-08-19.0 release was checked across the complete Singapore boundary, including the area east of the regional tile envelope. It contains 237,833 building records and 8,173 building-part records in this scope, excluding two marked underground. These are source records, not a verified count of real buildings.

The tile extract omitted 156 source records: one pavilion footprint and 155 building parts. All match the newer OpenStreetMap geometry exactly, with compatible source heights/storeys. They are restored, along with original detail for 506 simplified outlines; 21 other candidates retain their newer OSM corrections. The 125 restored parts with ground-based height/storey data can cast shadows. Another 29 elevated parts still require roof-elevation verification, and two records have no height.

Before construction-status filtering and future-scenario replacement, the assembled source data contain all 246,006 non-underground IDs from that original source reference. Native heights and floors are unchanged. This proves coverage of this dataset's records, not complete real-world buildings, current construction status or accurate heights. Unknown heights and unmodelled future projects remain explicit.
