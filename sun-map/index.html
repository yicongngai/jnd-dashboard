How to read this map

Sun direction, with
the assumptions in view.

The small centre dot marks the study location. The gold arc traces the sun’s compass direction from sunrise to sunset, with a moving sun marker. Click an hour label to change the time. The arc’s distance from the centre is schematic, not the sun’s distance or altitude; altitude is shown in the card and timeline chart. Bearings use true north. Date and time always use Singapore time (UTC+8).

  • Grey 3D buildings: source-recorded heights, not independently surveyed. Pale 3D buildings: storeys × 3 m. Ember buildings: a height you enter. Click any building to inspect its source.
  • Landed homes: source-classified houses and their linked building parts stay as flat footprints, with no 3D height or shadow. Click a footprint to see its available address.
  • Flat footprints: height is missing, an elevated part needs verification, or an overall outline has separate parts. Missing heights cast no shadow. Unshaded ground does not establish that a home receives sunlight.
  • The study follows your visible map automatically as you pan. Zoom in for individual buildings; the Singapore overview shows future-project markers. Shadows inside the dashed boundary use buildings from a wider buffer. Low-sun shadows are hidden if they could extend beyond that buffer.
  • Live shadows use a 2048-pixel ground mask; edge precision depends on zoom. Buildings are flat-roof prisms on level ground. Terrain, trees, balconies, roof slopes, clouds, interiors and shadows on façades are excluded. This is a neighbourhood ground-shadow estimate, not a unit-level sunlight certificate.
  • Blue-grey buildings show the completed-development scenario from reviewed vault and HDB plans, with approximate placement and storey-based heights. Filled teal dots have building models; hollow dots await alignment and cast no future shadows. Toggle future developments to compare. The solar date does not change construction status.
  • Existing footprints are a dated snapshot; new construction or demolished buildings may differ. Amber Park’s 21 storeys are verified from CDL, but 63 m remains an estimate.

Sun position uses geometric altitude from Astronomy Engine. Sunrise and sunset include its standard atmospheric refraction and solar-disc assumptions; actual visibility depends on weather and obstructions.
